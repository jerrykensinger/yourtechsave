Verizon vs. AT&T: compare the full family plan

Both carriers use multi-line pricing, offer device promotions, and reserve more hotspot and premium features for higher tiers. The useful comparison is the recurring household cost after discounts, add-ons, taxes and connected devices are included.

At a glance: Verizon's current myPlan family includes Unlimited Welcome, Unlimited Plus and Unlimited Ultimate. AT&T currently offers Value 2.0, Extra 2.0, Premium 2.0 and Elite 2.0. Both price service based in part on plan tier and line count.

Start with the line count, not the single-line headline

Both carriers use multi-line pricing, so compare the total service cost for your actual household. Verizon's price can also change with Auto Pay eligibility, optional perks and account discounts. AT&T advertises different per-line prices depending on plan tier and line count.

Perks are structured differently

Verizon's myPlan approach lets eligible lines add optional perks for entertainment, travel and other services. AT&T puts more of its premium benefits directly into higher plan tiers. A perk only has real value if it replaces something your household would otherwise pay for.

Hotspot use can separate the middle and premium tiers

Verizon Unlimited Plus currently includes 30GB of premium mobile hotspot. AT&T's current tiers range from 3GB on Value 2.0 to 250GB on Elite 2.0. Heavy hotspot users should compare the exact allowance on the plan they are considering rather than relying on the word "unlimited."

Device promotions can change the short-term math

Both carriers use device financing and promotional credits. Verizon notes that promotion eligibility can depend on staying on a qualifying plan, and AT&T device offers have their own eligibility rules. Separate the service price from the value and duration of device credits before deciding whether switching lowers the long-term cost.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • How many phone lines are on the account?
  • Which network performs better at home, work, school and frequent travel locations?
  • How much hotspot data does each line actually use?
  • Are optional entertainment or travel perks replacing services you already pay for?
  • Do you need watch or tablet lines?
  • Would switching cause you to lose existing device credits or discounts?
