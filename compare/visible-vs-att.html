Wireless comparison

Visible vs. AT&T: simple per-line pricing vs. a full family-plan lineup

Visible prices each line as its own plan on Verizon's network. AT&T uses line-count pricing across a broader postpaid lineup, with more hotspot and device-benefit options at higher tiers.

At a glance: Visible's standard monthly plans are $25, $35 and $45 per line with taxes and fees included. AT&T currently advertises four-line rates of $30, $40, $50 and $70 per line for Value 2.0, Extra 2.0, Premium 2.0 and Elite 2.0 after eligible AutoPay and paperless billing; AT&T taxes and fees are extra.

For four lines, compare the whole account

AT&T's current four-line advertised rates work out to $120 per month on Value 2.0, $160 on Extra 2.0, $200 on Premium 2.0 and $280 on Elite 2.0 before taxes and fees. Four Visible lines would be $100 on Visible, $140 on Visible+ or $180 on Visible+ Pro at the standard monthly rates shown here.

Those totals compare service rates only. Device financing, trade-in credits, insurance, watches, tablets, discounts and temporary promotions can change the real bill.

AT&T's line-count pricing can matter for households

AT&T states that the per-line price falls as more lines are added. Visible does not use a traditional shared family-plan discount structure; each line has its own plan. That makes the correct comparison dependent on how many lines you actually need.

AT&T offers a wider range of premium features

AT&T's higher tiers include substantially larger hotspot allowances, and Premium 2.0 and Elite 2.0 include unlimited high-speed phone data that does not slow based on how much the line uses. Elite 2.0 also adds international and connected-device benefits. Those features may be valuable for some households, but they also raise the service price.

Visible keeps the billing model simpler

Visible's standard monthly prices are the same whether you have one line or several, and listed taxes and fees are included. For a household trying to minimize recurring service cost without needing the broader postpaid feature set, that simplicity makes the math straightforward.

Device promotions can change a postpaid comparison

AT&T says customers do not need its most expensive unlimited plan to qualify for its best smartphone deals, although eligibility and offer terms vary. If a household frequently finances premium phones through the carrier, include the value and duration of device credits before deciding how much the service itself really costs.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • How many lines are you paying for?
  • Which network works better where your household spends time?
  • How much mobile hotspot data does each line actually use?
  • Are you receiving device trade-in or promotional bill credits today?
  • Do you need smartwatch, tablet, international or premium-data benefits?
  • Are taxes and fees included in the advertised price?
Before switching: calculate your current wireless cost per line, estimate family wireless savings, and use the carrier-switch checklist.
