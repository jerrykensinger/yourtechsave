How YourTechSave calculates and evaluates technology costs

The method is simple: separate the parts of a bill, show the math, label estimates, verify provider facts, and keep affiliate compensation outside recommendation criteria.

Methodology version 1.6 • Updated October 7, 2026

What the Tech Spending Checkup calculates

The current checkup uses values entered by the visitor and can also load YourTechSave’s public, source-linked wireless provider records for a directional benchmark when those records are still within the site’s freshness window. It does not connect to bank accounts, carrier accounts, or subscription services. The main calculations remain straightforward arithmetic.

Wireless service cost per line
Monthly wireless service cost ÷ number of phone lines paid for
Annual technology cost
Monthly recurring cost × 12
24-month cost
Monthly recurring cost × 24

Device financing is collected separately from wireless service so a phone payment is not accidentally presented as a plan expense that disappears when someone changes carriers.

Directional wireless comparison
Estimated current service-only cost shown beside up to three named, recently verified standard-plan examples for the same number of lines when fresh records are available

The examples are reference points, not automatic tier matches. Each example keeps its provider and plan name visible along with billing-term and tax/fee notes where available. Listed-price differences are not presented as guaranteed savings because eligibility, AutoPay, billing term, data features, taxes and fees, coverage and other conditions can change the real-world result.

Version note — v1.6: The checkup replaced a broad plan-price range with named fresh plan examples and added validation so estimated phone payments cannot exceed the wireless total entered.

What the tools do not claim

YourTechSave does not guarantee that switching a wireless or internet provider will save a specific amount.

Real savings depend on current pricing, taxes and fees, line count, promotions, eligibility, device financing, trade-in credits, coverage, features and other conditions. Calculations based directly on visitor-entered values are shown as arithmetic, not promises of future savings. When provider records are used, the result shows named fresh standard-price examples and any raw listed-price difference separately from a savings claim. The examples are not automatically matched to the visitor’s current plan tier.

How live provider data is handled

Wireless comparison records can include an official source URL, plan name, standard price or pricing structure, key plan features, last-verified date and any separate affiliate destination. The public comparison system uses a freshness threshold for time-sensitive records and can hide specific prices when the relevant data has not been reverified recently.

Temporary promotional offers are kept separate from standard pricing whenever practical. A short-lived introductory deal should not replace the normal price in the underlying comparison record unless it is explicitly labeled and its eligibility or expiration terms are recorded.

How home-internet availability is handled

Internet service is address-specific. A ZIP code can help organize a local search, but YourTechSave does not treat ZIP-level information as proof that a provider serves a particular home. The Home Internet Finder therefore distinguishes options that are worth checking from options confirmed available.

For independent local discovery, YourTechSave points visitors to the FCC National Broadband Map, which reports providers and technologies at individual serviceable locations based on provider filings. Provider-specific availability should then be confirmed directly with the provider before ordering.

Broad fixed-wireless and satellite providers can also have structured provider records with official source URLs, plan names, regular pricing where it can be cleanly verified, facts, promotions and last-verified dates. Home-internet records use the same 7-day freshness principle as wireless records: when a record becomes stale, the public page suppresses specific time-sensitive prices and directs visitors to the official availability checker.

Recommendation criteria

Comparisons should focus on consumer needs such as coverage experience, number of lines, total service cost, hotspot use, international needs, device financing and other relevant features. Affiliate availability and commission amount are stored separately from those criteria and are not intended to determine recommendation order.

Source hierarchy

For provider-specific pricing, plan terms and features, YourTechSave prefers current official provider pages or official support documentation. Secondary sources can provide context but should not replace a current primary source for a provider's own terms when the primary source is available.

Corrections

Technology pricing changes quickly. If you find a current factual error, email contact@yourtechsave.com. Material corrections should be reflected in the affected page rather than preserving an outdated claim.
