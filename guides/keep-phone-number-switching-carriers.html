Number porting

Can you keep your phone number when switching carriers?

In many carrier switches, yes. The process is called number porting, and accurate account information is important to a smooth transfer.

Published October 3, 2026 • YourTechSave wireless savings series

What the FCC says

The Federal Communications Commission explains that if you switch service providers and remain in the same geographic area, you can keep an existing phone number. Number portability can apply across wireless, wireline and IP providers. Exact eligibility and the transfer process can depend on the circumstances.

What information should you have ready?

The new provider may ask for information that matches the existing account, such as the phone number being transferred, account number, billing ZIP code and a passcode or transfer PIN when applicable. Use the current carrier's instructions because security procedures vary.

Why matching information matters

A mismatch in the account number, ZIP code or security information can delay a transfer. Before starting, review the existing carrier account and make sure the name and account information you provide are accurate.

What about your old service?

Follow the new carrier's porting instructions rather than treating a number transfer like an ordinary cancellation. The phone number itself is being moved between providers, so timing matters. Keep access to the old account until the process is complete and review the final bill afterward.

Does porting the number move everything else?

No. Moving the number does not automatically move voicemail messages, device financing, insurance, cloud backups or other account features. Back up anything important and review those items separately.
