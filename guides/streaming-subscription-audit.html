Subscription guide

How to audit streaming subscriptions without canceling everything

The goal is not to eliminate entertainment. It is to find recurring charges that no longer provide enough value.

Updated October 3, 2026 • About 5 minutes

Make one list before making any changes

Start with every recurring video, music, cloud, gaming or app subscription you can identify. Record the normal monthly cost even if the service bills annually. A scattered subscription picture makes small charges easy to ignore.

Classify each service by actual use

For a quick audit, use three categories: frequent, sometimes and rarely. This is more useful than simply counting how many subscriptions you have. A frequently used $20 service may provide more value than a rarely used $8 service.

Annual subscription cost
Monthly price × 12

A $12 monthly service is $144 per year. Three rarely used services at $12 each add up to $432 per year. Annualizing the price makes the trade-off more visible.

Check bundles before canceling

Some services are included through a wireless plan, internet package, credit-card benefit or other bundle. Canceling the wrong piece can change the economics of another service. Confirm whether the subscription is billed directly and whether removing it changes another discount.

Look for duplicate purpose

Households can end up with multiple services that serve the same role. That does not automatically make one wasteful, but it creates a useful question: are both being used enough to justify their combined annual cost?

Consider rotation instead of permanent cancellation

If you use a service only when a particular show or sports season is active, periodic subscription rotation can be more practical than paying all year. Before canceling, review whether there are restart fees, annual commitments or promotional terms that would be lost.

A simple subscription review checklist

  • List every recurring service and its normal price.
  • Mark each as frequent, sometimes or rarely used.
  • Annualize the cost of the rarely used group.
  • Check bundles and family usage before canceling.
  • Review annual plans and cancellation terms.
  • Revisit the list every few months.
