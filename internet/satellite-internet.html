Satellite internet

Satellite internet is not one technology anymore.

Starlink, Hughesnet and Viasat can all reach homes beyond many wired networks, but their satellite architecture, latency, equipment and plan structures differ.

Verified against official provider sources October 3, 2026

Use satellite as part of the local search, not as an automatic last resort. In some rural areas it may be one of the strongest options available. In areas with reliable fiber or cable, the trade-offs can look very different.

Low-Earth orbit vs. traditional satellite

Starlink uses a large low-Earth-orbit constellation. The shorter path between the home and orbit is designed to reduce latency compared with traditional geostationary satellite systems. Hughesnet and Viasat use satellite systems built around different architectures and service models; Hughesnet also offers a Fusion option in select areas that combines satellite and terrestrial wireless technologies for more responsive traffic.

Latency matters differently from download speed

Advertised download speed does not tell the whole story. Real-time gaming, remote desktop work, video calls and other interactive applications can be more sensitive to latency than ordinary web browsing or video streaming.

Do not ignore equipment and installation

Satellite service can involve a dish, gateway or other hardware. Compare upfront hardware cost, rental or lease charges, professional-installation requirements, return obligations and cancellation terms along with the monthly service price.

Data policies deserve special attention

Providers can describe data differently. Hughesnet distinguishes Priority Data from Standard Data, while other providers may use network-management or plan-specific policies. Read the current terms instead of treating the word “unlimited” as identical across providers.
